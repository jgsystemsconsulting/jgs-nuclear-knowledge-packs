# Chapter 2: The Non-LWR PDC Adaptation Framework

Sources: NRC RG 1.232 Rev 0, Developing Principal Design Criteria for Non-Light Water Reactors, April 2018, ML17325A611; verified via Federal Register notice 83 FR 15178 on 2026-11-13.

## Core Idea

The adaptation approach treats the 10 CFR Part 50 Appendix A general design criteria set as the baseline structure and works it criterion-area by criterion-area for a non-LWR technology. For each area the writer judges whether the area applies, records an adaptation outcome, and keeps the basis so the finished PDC set stays traceable to the GDC baseline. Technology-specific phenomena the GDC never addressed are where new or replaced criteria come from. This chapter stays at methodology-pattern depth; no appendix criterion text is restated.

## Frameworks Introduced

- Criterion-area adaptation. The method walks the GDC baseline area by area rather than adopting the full set unchanged or inventing a free-standing criteria list with no baseline link.
- Adaptation outcomes. Each area receives one recorded outcome: carry a criterion over, adapt its wording, replace it with a technology-specific criterion, or judge it inapplicable.
- Traceability discipline. The adaptation basis is recorded so a reviewer can trace every PDC back to its GDC area. Traceability is documentation discipline, not optional commentary.
- Numbering families. A non-LWR PDC set keeps its own numbering family while staying traceable to the GDC baseline. RG 1.232 appendix exemplars use family labels such as SFR-DC, MHTGR-DC, and MSR-DC; the labels name families, not individual criteria.

## Key Concepts

- Applicability question: does this GDC area apply to the non-LWR technology under review, given its coolants, spectrum, fuel form, and barriers?
- Carry over: the area applies and the baseline criterion can be retained for the PDC set without technology-driven change.
- Adapt wording: the area applies, but wording must change so the criterion fits the non-LWR technology.
- Replace with a technology-specific criterion: the area still matters, yet the baseline text cannot carry the technology, so a technology-specific criterion takes its place.
- Judge inapplicable: the area does not apply to the technology; the outcome and basis are still recorded.
- New or replaced criteria from technology-specific phenomena: where the GDC set never addressed a phenomenon the non-LWR design raises, new or replaced criteria arise from that gap, not from free invention detached from the baseline walk.
- Traceability to GDC baseline: each PDC entry stays linked to the GDC area it came from, including inapplicable judgments, so the set can be reviewed against the baseline structure.
- Numbering family: the PDC set uses its own family label and numbering (for example SFR-DC, MHTGR-DC, or MSR-DC at the appendix-exemplar level) rather than reusing GDC numbers as if the text were unchanged.

## Mental Models

Picture a checklist walk across the GDC baseline. At each area the writer answers applicability, picks one outcome, and writes the basis. The finished PDC set is the record of that walk, not a parallel catalog invented beside the GDC. Numbering may look technology-native (SFR-DC, MHTGR-DC, MSR-DC family labels), yet every entry still points back to a GDC area. Gaps the GDC never covered are filled by new or replaced criteria only after the baseline walk shows the gap.

## Anti-patterns

Skipping the baseline walk and drafting free-standing criteria with no GDC-area link is the chapter's named anti-pattern: the method is structured adaptation, not a blank-page criteria catalog. A companion error is adopting every GDC unchanged and calling the result a non-LWR PDC set. A third error is recording outcomes without bases, so a reviewer cannot trace the set. A fourth error is restating appendix criterion text, numbers, titles, or counts; those stay outside this pack.

## Key Takeaways

- Adapt the Appendix A GDC baseline criterion-area by criterion-area; do not adopt the GDC unchanged and do not invent an untraceable set.
- Record one outcome per area: carry over, adapt wording, replace with a technology-specific criterion, or judge inapplicable, with the basis written down.
- Technology-specific phenomena the GDC never addressed are where new or replaced criteria come from.
- Keep the PDC set in its own numbering family while remaining traceable to the GDC baseline.
- No appendix criterion text, numbers, titles, or counts are packaged here.
