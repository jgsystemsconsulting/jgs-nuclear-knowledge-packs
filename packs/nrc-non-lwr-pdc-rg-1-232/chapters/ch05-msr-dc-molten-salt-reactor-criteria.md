# Chapter 5: MSR-DC, Molten Salt Reactor Design Criteria Family

Sources: NRC RG 1.232 Rev 0, Developing Principal Design Criteria for Non-Light Water Reactors, April 2018, ML17325A611; verified via Federal Register notice 83 FR 15178 on 2026-11-13.

## Core Idea

RG 1.232 Appendix C carries the molten salt reactor design criteria (MSR-DC) as one technology-family exemplar. At family level the set shows where liquid fuel or liquid coolant salt, online salt chemistry and processing, and salt-system barriers change the adaptation questions a reviewer asks. The number, titles, and text of criteria inside Appendix C are not on record in this repo and are not stated in this pack. No individual criterion is named, numbered, titled, counted, or quoted here. Designs outside the three exemplar families must develop their own PDC by the ch02 GDC-baseline adaptation approach; the exemplar sets illustrate the approach and do not bound it. The three families are never ranked and never blended.

## Frameworks Introduced

- MSR-DC family label. MSR-DC names the molten salt reactor numbering family used as an RG 1.232 Appendix C exemplar. The label marks a family, not any single criterion.
- Family-level adaptation focus. The chapter frames the class of GDC-area questions the MSR technology raises under the ch02 adaptation walk, without restating appendix text.
- Outside-family route. A non-LWR design that is not SFR, MHTGR, or MSR still needs its own principal design criteria. The route is the same ch02 GDC-baseline adaptation approach; the three exemplars do not limit which technologies may use it.
- Parallel exemplars only. SFR-DC, MHTGR-DC, and MSR-DC sit side by side as illustrations. They are never ranked and never blended into one mixed set.

## Key Concepts

- Molten salt reactor (MSR): a non-LWR technology class that uses liquid fuel salt or liquid coolant salt, with online salt chemistry and processing and salt-system barriers in the design picture.
- MSR-DC: the RG 1.232 Appendix C exemplar family for molten salt reactor principal design criteria, treated here only as a family label.
- Liquid fuel or liquid coolant salt: salt inventory that is liquid in normal operation and therefore changes which GDC areas apply and what wording those areas need.
- Online salt chemistry and processing: continuous or near-continuous chemistry control and salt processing that raise adaptation questions the LWR GDC baseline did not frame for solid-fuel water-cooled designs.
- Salt-system barriers: barrier roles carried by salt systems and their boundaries, which shift containment and related GDC-area questions relative to LWR practice.
- Outside the three families: any non-LWR design that is not covered by the SFR, MHTGR, or MSR exemplars still develops PDC by the ch02 method; the exemplars illustrate and do not bound.
- Family-level statement only: the chapter classifies where adaptation questions arise for this technology class. It never decides a real design's PDC set.

## Mental Models

Treat Appendix C as a worked example of the ch02 walk applied to one technology family, not as a criterion catalog this pack holds. Ask where liquid salt (fuel or coolant), online salt chemistry and processing, and salt-system barriers force a different applicability answer or a different adaptation outcome than an LWR baseline would. Keep every answer at that class-of-questions depth. When the design sits outside SFR, MHTGR, and MSR, return to ch02 and run the baseline walk for that technology; do not stretch an exemplar family to cover it. When the question names a specific MSR-DC criterion, number, title, or count, the pack has no on-record answer and routes the reader to RG 1.232 itself.

## Anti-patterns

Inventing or restating individual MSR-DC criterion text, numbers, titles, or counts is the chapter's named anti-pattern: those details are not on record here. A companion error is reading this family-level chapter as a finished PDC set for any real molten salt reactor. A third error is treating the three exemplar families as a closed list of allowed technologies, or ordering them by preference, or blending their adaptation questions into one mixed set. A fourth error is forcing an outside-family design into an exemplar label instead of running the ch02 walk for that design's own phenomena.
