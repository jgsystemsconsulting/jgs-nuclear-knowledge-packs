# Chapter 5: Defense-in-Depth Adequacy Evaluation

Sources: NRC RG 1.233 Rev 0, Guidance for a Technology-Inclusive, Risk-Informed, and Performance-Based Methodology to Inform the Licensing Basis and Content of Applications for Licenses, Certifications, and Approvals for Non-Light Water Reactors, June 2020, ML20091L698; verified via Federal Register notice 85 FR 35339 on 2026-11-13.

## Core Idea

The defense-in-depth adequacy evaluation considers two faces together: plant capability defense-in-depth (what the design provides) and programmatic defense-in-depth (what plant programs provide). Plant capability covers barriers, redundancy, diversity, margins, and inherent and passive features. Programmatic defense-in-depth covers quality assurance, testing, maintenance, and risk management activities. The integrated judgement is a check that risk-informed event selection and SSC classification have not eroded depth, including where uncertainty or incomplete PRA scope weakens risk insight. The method presumes a PRA of quality adequate to the use made of it, complemented by deterministic and engineering judgement where PRA scope or uncertainty is limited. No evaluation method, checklist, scoring, or pass condition is stated in this pack. No evaluation is performed here, and no design passes or fails in this chapter.

## Frameworks Introduced

- Two faces, one check. Plant capability and programmatic defense-in-depth are evaluated together, not as optional alternatives.
- Plant capability face. Barriers, redundancy, diversity, margins, and inherent and passive features name what the design provides at outline depth.
- Programmatic face. Quality assurance, testing, maintenance, and risk management activities name what plant programs provide at outline depth.
- Integrated adequacy judgement. The stage checks whether event selection and classification stripped away depth; it is a check, not a scored verdict method packaged here.
- Complementary inputs under uncertainty. Where PRA scope or uncertainty is limited, deterministic and engineering judgement complement risk insights; PRA quality adequate to the use made of it is presumed, not demonstrated, in this pack.

## Key Concepts

- Defense-in-depth: layered capability and programmatic provisions that keep risk-informed selections from standing on a single thin line of protection.
- Plant capability defense-in-depth: what the design provides, including barriers, redundancy, diversity, margins, and inherent and passive features.
- Programmatic defense-in-depth: what plant programs provide, including quality assurance, testing, maintenance, and risk management activities.
- Integrated adequacy judgement: joint look at both faces as a check on event selection and classification outcomes.
- Erosion check: the question whether risk-informed selections removed depth the design and programs still need; asked at outline depth only.
- PRA quality presumption: the method presumes PRA quality fits the use made of the PRA; this pack states the presumption only.
- Deterministic and engineering judgement complement: where PRA scope or uncertainty is limited, judgement fills beside risk insight rather than erasing it.
- Fence: no evaluation method, scoring, pass condition, or design verdict is stated in this pack.

## Mental Models

Picture two ledgers read side by side after events are selected and SSCs are classified. One ledger lists what the plant hardware and inherent features supply. The other lists what programs supply. Adequacy here means reading both ledgers together and asking whether the earlier risk-informed choices hollowed either ledger out. When the risk picture is incomplete, judgement still sits beside it; the chapter does not score the ledgers or stamp a design result.

## Anti-patterns

Treating defense-in-depth as plant capability alone, or as programs alone, is the named anti-pattern: the stage holds both faces together. A companion error is reading this chapter as a checklist, scoring method, or pass-or-fail procedure for a real design. A third error is issuing an adequacy verdict, or claiming a design passes or fails, from these notes. A fourth error is dropping the PRA-quality presumption or the deterministic complement and treating thin risk insight as enough on its own inside this outline.

## Key Takeaways

- Defense-in-depth adequacy evaluation considers plant capability and programmatic faces together.
- Plant capability includes barriers, redundancy, diversity, margins, and inherent and passive features.
- Programmatic defense-in-depth includes quality assurance, testing, maintenance, and risk management activities.
