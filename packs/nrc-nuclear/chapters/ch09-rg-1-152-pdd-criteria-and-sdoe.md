# Chapter 9: Criteria for programmable digital devices and the secure development and operational environment

Sources: S8 Regulatory Guide 1.152, "Criteria for Programmable Digital Devices in Safety-Related Systems of Nuclear Power Plants" (Rev. 4, July 2023, ML23054A463), sections A. INTRODUCTION (Purpose through Related Guidance), B. DISCUSSION, and C. STAFF REGULATORY GUIDANCE, lines 11-111 and 138-587 of `sources/text/S8.txt` (printed pp. 1-2, 4-12). Exclusions per the P18 slice pin: cover masthead above A. INTRODUCTION; Purpose of Regulatory Guides, Paperwork Reduction, and Public Protection plates; D. IMPLEMENTATION; REFERENCES. Name-only standard cited with the edition printed in this source: IEEE Std 7-4.3.2-2016. The 2013 family's citation of IEEE Std 7-4.3.2-2003 stays in ch02-ch07 and in the ch08 family row. ## Core Idea

Regulatory Guide 1.152 Revision 4 states an approach the NRC staff finds acceptable for promoting high functional reliability, design quality, and a secure development and operational environment (SDOE) for programmable digital devices (PDDs) in safety-related systems of nuclear power plants. The guide endorses IEEE Std 7-4.3.2-2016 in part, with named exceptions and clarifications. It is a criteria and environment guide for PDDs, not a substitute for the July 2013 software life-cycle Regulatory Guide family or for BTP 7-14 plan and product review.

Revision 4 changes the guide's title. Earlier revisions used "Criteria for Use of Computers in Safety Systems of Nuclear Power Plants." Revision 4 uses "Criteria for Programmable Digital Devices in Safety-Related Systems of Nuclear Power Plants." Pin the 2023 title and Revision 4; do not restore the older wording.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **PDD criteria path (S8 A Purpose; B Reason for Revision; C opening).** The RG describes methods acceptable for meeting regulatory requirements on reliability, design quality, and SDOE for PDDs in safety-related systems. It endorses IEEE Std 7-4.3.2-2016 as an adequate basis for those topics subject to the staff exceptions and clarifications in Section C. The staff finds the 2016 revision acceptable supplemental guidance on how to meet the IEEE Std 603-1991 criteria incorporated by reference in 10 CFR 50.55a(h), again subject to those exceptions and clarifications.
- **Title and scope shift (S8 cover; B Reason for Revision).** Revision 4 removes prior SDOE text that lived inside the RG itself and instead endorses, with clarifications, the SDOE criteria inside IEEE Std 7-4.3.2-2016. It adds fault-detection and self-diagnostics guidance for DI&C systems when those features are used. It endorses Annex D of the 2016 standard (hazards identification and control) with clarifications, and it clarifies how control-of-access guidance applies to safety-related PDDs.
- **Regulatory skeleton named by the RG (S8 A Applicable Regulations; Related Guidance).** The guide ties to 10 CFR Part 50 and Part 52 applicants and licensees; 10 CFR 50.55a(h) protection and safety systems (IEEE Std 603-1991 path, with older construction-permit cohorts as the RG states); GDC 13 instrumentation and control; GDC 21 protection system reliability and testability; and Appendix B Criterion III design control. Related guidance the RG names includes Commission policy on common-cause failure in digital I&C and staff diversity and defense-in-depth evaluation guidance. Those related documents are not restated here.
- **Endorsement shape: exceptions first (S8 C.1.a).** Two standing exceptions:
  1. The staff has not endorsed Annexes B, C, and E of IEEE Std 7-4.3.2-2016. The staff still treats those annexes as useful information only.
  2. Examples inside the 2016 standard are not automatically plant-applicable. Endorsement of the standard is not a determination that every example fits every licensee. The licensee or applicant must confirm applicability before relying on an example.
- **Clarification block: system integrity and hazards (S8 C.1.b(1)).** Safety-related I&C that uses PDDs should behave in a predictable and repeatable way. The staff finds Annex D of IEEE Std 7-4.3.2-2016 acceptable for identifying and controlling hazards of DI&C devices in safety-related systems, with three clarifications: Annex D scope is limited to DI&C devices; example hazard-analysis techniques named in the annex are examples only and are not themselves endorsed as exclusive methods; Annex D's background discussion of Annex C does not revive Annex C, which remains unendorsed.
- **Self-diagnostics and fault detection (S8 C.1.b 1.2).** Where self-diagnostics are used for timely failure detection, the staff accepts the standard's criteria and adds supplements: watchdog-timer lock-up detection for microprocessor-based devices must not share the failure mode of the monitored processor and should drive a predetermined fail-safe output, preferably via hardware-based watchdog functions; integrated self-diagnostics must preserve channel independence, system integrity, and the single-failure criterion; diagnostic hardware and software safety classification must match the tested system unless full independence keeps a diagnostic failure from inhibiting the safety function; detected failures must align with single-failure and FMEA assumptions; diagnostic design should minimize added complexity; diagnostics are verified in periodic functional tests; and self-diagnostics may reduce or replace manual channel operability tests only when those design rules hold and when execution is confirmed in operation, automatic test equipment remains testable, and administrative controls periodically verify diagnostic performance.
- **Priority functions (S8 C.1.b 1.3).** When priority functions mediate commands from safety-related and non-safety-related PDDs to a safety-related component, the licensee or applicant must analyze the implementation for new hazards from increased interconnectivity. The RG does not dictate whether the priority function lives inside the safety PDD or in a separate device.
- **Independence and communication (S8 C.1.b(2)).** Digital communication raises propagational-failure hazard. Designs need enough independence to stop fault propagation from non-safety-related systems into safety-related systems, between redundant safety portions, and from design-basis events into the safety-related system, and to keep redundancy effective. The staff accepts the standard's independence criteria and adds supplements on interdivisional communication (no outside message may redirect a safety processor's instruction sequence unless associated safety functions are bypassed, tripped, or out of service), bandwidth sufficient to avoid congestion, and a definition of point-to-point communication that keeps intermediate equipment inside the sending or receiving division. Multidivisional control and display stations that can affect safety-related equipment (including bypass, lockout, block, and inhibit) get further supplements so control and communication processors only act on commands that pass error checking.
- **Control of access and SDOE (S8 C.1.b(3)).** The staff accepts the standard's control-of-access and independence criteria for protection against non-malicious acts (undesirable behavior of connected systems, unintended access). Licensees and applicants provide an SDOE vulnerability assessment per the standard's control-of-access clause and identify measures or design features for the vulnerabilities found. Cybersecurity features must not be implemented directly in safety-related systems in a way that harms safety performance, effectiveness, reliability, or operation. Pre-installation receipt, storage, staging, and testing of safety-related PDDs should occur in a secure environment. This RG does not address control-of-access features meant to stop malicious cyberattacks; that topic sits under 10 CFR 73.54 and the separate cybersecurity regulatory guide the RG names. When a submittal describes cybersecurity design features, staff review of those features is limited to confirming they do not degrade safety-related reliability or safety function capability. Design-certification applicants should still keep safety-related I&C from presenting an electronic path that enables unauthorized access to the plant's safety-related system (a hardware-based unidirectional device is one approach the staff would consider).
- **Common-cause failure pointer (S8 C.1.b(4)).** PDDs can be more susceptible to CCF from hardware defects or latent software or logic defects. CCF can remove a safety function when it is needed or can cause spurious actuation. The staff accepts the standard's CCF criteria and points to separate staff diversity and defense-in-depth evaluation guidance for latent design defects in digital safety-related systems. That evaluation guidance is outside this chapter.
- **Commercial digital equipment (S8 C.1.b(5)).** The staff accepts the standard's criteria for commercial digital equipment in safety-related systems. The standard's informative annex on commercial-grade dedication remains unendorsed; dedication practice is pointed to other NRC commercial-grade dedication guides the RG names, not reconstructed here.
- **Annex map in the RG's own words (S8 B annex list).** Annex A is a mapping aid with no requirements. Annex B (diversity) is not endorsed. Annex C (commercial computers) is not endorsed as guidance. Annex D (hazards) is endorsed with the clarifications above. Annex E (communication independence) is not endorsed; normative communication independence criteria are endorsed with clarifications. Annex F was deleted in the standard. Annexes G and H are glossary and bibliography only.
- **Secondary-reference rule (S8 B Documents Discussed).** Codes or standards the RG endorses may cite further documents. A secondary reference is binding only if incorporated into NRC regulations, or is an accepted method only if a regulatory guide endorses it. Otherwise it is neither a requirement nor a generic NRC-approved method, though licensees may still use it with justification consistent with current practice and applicable requirements.
- **International standards note (S8 B).** The RG considered named IAEA and IEC publications during development and does not specifically endorse them for the purpose of this guide.

## Key Concepts

- **PDD means software or programmable logic does the function.** Computers, programmable hardware, and firmware devices are in scope as the RG uses the term.
- **Endorsement is partial.** Exceptions and clarifications are NRC staff positions. They are not a license to paste IEEE clause text into pack notes.
- **SDOE is environment control for development and operation.** Vulnerability assessment, secure pre-installation handling, and non-malicious access control sit here. Malicious cyberattack programs sit under a different regulation and guide.
- **Predictable and repeatable behavior is the integrity target.** Same inputs and conditions should yield the same outputs; integrity design, hazards control, diagnostics, and independence all serve that target.
- **Self-diagnostics are optional credit, not free credit.** Using them to thin manual tests requires the full design and administrative package the RG lists.
- **2016 is this chapter's IEEE edition.** The 2013 RG family and ch08 family row stay on IEEE Std 7-4.3.2-2003. The owner's page for the 2016 edition is not in the signpost; the 2003 row is the one the signpost carries.
- **Licensing basis decides the binding revision for a plant.** This pack pins RG 1.152 Rev 4 and the 2016 IEEE edition that Rev 4 endorses. A plant committed to another revision is reviewed against that commitment.

## Mental Models

- **ch09 is the criteria sheet; ch10 is the staff exam rubric.** This chapter states what the RG accepts. The appendix that tells reviewers how to evaluate an applicant's claim of conformance to IEEE Std 7-4.3.2 lives in ch10.
- **Exceptions are walls; clarifications are handrails.** Unendorsed annexes stay out. Endorsed clauses still get staff supplements where the RG writes them.
- **SDOE is the locked shop floor; cybersecurity is the alarm system.** Same plant, different rule sets. Do not merge the checklists.
- **Name the IEEE edition the source prints.** Do not upgrade ch02-ch07 to 2016 because this chapter uses 2016.

## Anti-patterns

- **Restoring the pre-Rev 4 title in pack prose.** Revision 4 renamed the guide; pin the 2023 title.
- **Treating endorsement of IEEE Std 7-4.3.2-2016 as endorsement of every annex and every example.** Annexes B, C, and E are out; examples need plant-specific fit.
- **Quoting or reconstructing IEEE clause text to explain an NRC exception.** Restate the NRC position; name the designation and the 2016 edition; stop.
- **Using self-diagnostics to drop channel tests without the RG's design and administrative conditions.**
- **Folding malicious cybersecurity design into this RG's SDOE body.** The RG separates them.
- **Silent upgrade of the 2013 family from IEEE Std 7-4.3.2-2003 to 2016.** Edition follows the source each chapter slices.
- **Assuming this pack's pin is every plant's licensing basis.**

## Key Takeaways

1. RG 1.152 Rev 4 (July 2023, ML23054A463) is the pack body for PDD criteria and SDOE; title and pin are the 2023 programmable-digital-device wording.
2. The RG endorses IEEE Std 7-4.3.2-2016 in part. Staff exceptions withhold endorsement of Annexes B, C, and E and withhold blanket applicability of the standard's examples.
3. Staff clarifications cover system integrity and Annex D hazards control, self-diagnostics and watchdog behavior, priority functions, communication independence, multidivisional stations, SDOE vulnerability assessment and secure pre-installation handling, CCF pointing to separate diversity review guidance, and commercial digital equipment without endorsing Annex C.
4. SDOE here addresses non-malicious development and operational environment controls; malicious cyberattack protection is a different regulatory path the RG names but does not own.
5. IEEE Std 7-4.3.2 stays name-only. This chapter cites the 2016 edition because that is what Rev 4 prints. The 2013 family remains on 2003. The signpost carries the 2003 owner row, not a 2016 row.
6. The plant licensing basis, not this pack, decides which revision binds a given plant.
