# Chapter 10: Staff evaluation of the application of IEEE Std 7-4.3.2

Sources: S9 NUREG-0800, Appendix 7.1-D, "Guidance for Evaluation of the Application of IEEE Std 7-4.3.2" (Rev. 1, August 2016, ML16019A114), sections 1 through 9 (Areas of Review through Secure Development and Operational Environment), lines 54-1117 of `sources/text/S9.txt` (printed pp. 2-22). Name-only standard cited with the edition printed in this appendix: IEEE Std 7-4.3.2-2003. The 2016 endorsement lives in ch09 and is not imported here. The plant licensing basis, not this pack, decides which revision binds a given plant.

## Core Idea

Appendix 7.1-D tells NRC staff how to evaluate an applicant's or licensee's claim that a computer-based safety I&C design applies IEEE Std 7-4.3.2. It is reviewer guidance inside NUREG-0800 Chapter 7. It is not a second copy of RG 1.152. The RG states criteria the staff finds acceptable; this appendix walks the standard's clause order and states what the reviewer examines, what evidence counts, and where the appendix adds no digital-specific guidance beyond IEEE Std 603-1991 and its companion evaluation appendix.

The August 2016 appendix prints IEEE Std 7-4.3.2-2003 (and names the 1982 ancestor once for history). This chapter cites 2003. RG 1.152 Rev 4's endorsement of the 2016 edition is ch09. Do not upgrade this chapter's citation to 2016 to force the two chapters to match.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Regulatory setup the reviewer starts from (S9 §1).** 10 CFR 50.55a(h) sets the protection and safety system path: older construction-permit cohorts may stay on their licensing basis or meet IEEE Std 603-1991 (with the January 30, 1995 correction sheet) or, for the middle cohort, IEEE Std 279-1971 or IEEE Std 603-1991; applications filed on or after May 13, 1999 that do not reference a final design approval or design certification must meet IEEE Std 603-1991 and that correction sheet. IEEE Std 603-1991 does not itself detail digital computers; the digital supplement path the appendix records runs through IEEE Std 7-4.3.2-2003, with RG 1.152 providing guidance on applying safety system criteria to computer-based safety systems and endorsing IEEE Std 7-4.3.2-2003 as this appendix prints it. IEEE Std 603-1991 remains the regulation's requirement even when the 2003 standard references a later IEEE Std 603 edition. Secondary codes and standards bind only if incorporated into regulations, or count as accepted methods only if a regulatory guide endorses them; otherwise they may be used with justification consistent with current practice.
- **Scope of the evaluation (S9 §2).** IEEE Std 7-4.3.2-2003 and RG 1.152 cover computer-based safety I&C systems (SAR Sections 7.2 through 7.6). The standard amplifies IEEE Std 603-1991 for computers as safety system components. Used with IEEE Std 603-1991, it sets minimum functional and design criteria for those computers. Criteria can also be applied, on a graded basis, to nonsafety digital I&C with high importance to safety (SAR 7.7 and 7.8). Data communications (SAR 7.9) inherit the criteria of the principal I&C systems they support. The appendix notes that RG 1.152 did not endorse the standard's annexes, yet some appendix subsections still draw review guidance related to annex topics.
- **No extra definitions (S9 §3).** The appendix adds no definitions beyond those in IEEE Std 7-4.3.2-2003.
- **Design basis clause (S9 §4).** Review of each safety system's design basis stays with IEEE Std 603-1991 Clause 4 and the companion IEEE Std 603 evaluation appendix. This appendix adds no separate digital design-basis rubric at that clause.
- **Safety system criteria walk (S9 §5 opening).** Clause 5 of IEEE Std 603-1991 requires safety systems to hold plant parameters inside design-basis limits with precision and reliability. Appendix 7.1-D follows the IEEE Std 7-4.3.2-2003 clause order. Where the digital standard adds nothing beyond IEEE Std 603-1991, the reviewer is sent to the matching IEEE Std 603 evaluation subsection.
- **Single-failure and digital common-cause concern (S9 §5.1).** Single-failure requirements remain those of IEEE Std 603-1991. The appendix flags digital-specific staff concerns: shared databases and process equipment can propagate common-cause failure across redundant equipment, and software programming errors can defeat hardware redundancy. Staff therefore emphasize defense-in-depth against common-cause failures within and between functions. Detailed diversity and defense-in-depth evaluation methods live in separate SRP guidance outside this appendix's body and outside this pack chapter.
- **Completion of protective action (S9 §5.2).** No digital add-on here; use the IEEE Std 603 evaluation subsection.
- **Quality and software life-cycle evidence (S9 §5.3).** Confirm Appendix B QA applies to the safety system; QA program adequacy is reviewed with SAR Chapter 17. For digital computer-based systems, address the quality criteria the 2003 standard states for Clause 5.3. Hardware quality traces to IEEE Std 603-1991; software quality traces to the life-cycle process standard the appendix names. The appendix points the reviewer at BTP 7-14 for software development features to examine, with three objectives: plans that support a high-quality software life cycle exist; the plans are followed; and the life cycle produces acceptable design outputs. Software tool control under configuration management, verification that tools are suitable, independent V&V organization and problem-report closure, configuration management baselines and responsibility splits, and software project risk management (problem prevention, impact, and which risks to treat) are all in the reviewer's quality lens. Metrics, requirements, design, implementation, integration, validation, and installation activity groups leave traceable records. Security in the life-cycle sense includes establishing a secure development and operational environment. Safety characteristics focus on software effects on system hazards and controls on those hazards.
- **Equipment qualification (S9 §5.4).** Environmental qualification for safety-related computer-based I&C follows the dedicated environmental qualification regulatory guide the appendix names, on top of IEEE Std 603-1991. Computer system testing for qualification must run with software and diagnostics representative of actual operation and must exercise every portion needed for safety functions or whose failure could impair them (memory, CPU, I/O, displays, diagnostics, communications, interfaces as appropriate). Qualification of existing commercial computers uses the commercial-grade evaluation guidance the appendix names (including PLC-specific guidance) together with BTP 7-14 for vendor process review. Acceptance needs documented evidence that hardware, software, firmware, and interfaces can perform required functions. Where traditional qualification cannot be applied, commercial-grade dedication verifies equivalence to Appendix B quality; dedication covers hardware, software, and firmware needed for the safety function and includes design-process evaluation for software and firmware.
- **System integrity (S9 §5.5).** Beyond IEEE Std 603-1991 integrity expectations, the digital path needs design for computer integrity, design for test and calibration, and fault detection and self-diagnostics.
- **Independence and communications (S9 §5.6).** Data communications between safety channels or between safety and nonsafety systems must not inhibit the safety function. Equipment used for both safety and nonsafety functions is classified as part of the safety system, including software. Nonsafety software on a safety computer is safety-related software with the attendant requirements, including communications isolation from other nonsafety software. GDC 24 separation of protection and control applies. Practical review targets: the safety system must not need nonsafety input to perform its safety function; failure of the nonsafety system, the communications path, or nonsafety data must not prevent or bias the safety determination; the online safety determination path should not take influence from nonsafety systems. Example acceptable patterns the appendix offers (not an exhaustive list) include one-way broadcast from safety to nonsafety without handshaking, and carefully examined two-way schemes that keep communications deterministic, bound shared-memory use, and handle stale, garbled, or failed-link data. Reviewers who lack communications depth should get help rather than guess.
- **Test, calibration, and diagnostics caution (S9 §5.7).** Test and calibration functions must not impair the computer's safety function. Self-diagnostics help find partial failures that operators might miss, but diagnostic code has been a real failure source; reviewers examine FMEA treatment of diagnostic failures and distrust claims that diagnostics make operational-code failure non-credible. Large diagnostic suites raise total software complexity; balance that cost against confidence gained, including credit for prior extensive use of mature diagnostics versus new operational code. A non-software (hardware) watchdog timer matters: a software-only watchdog dies if the processor freezes. Prefer a hardware watchdog whose only software touch is a reset after the safety processor completes its function, and confirm software cannot jump to that reset and neuter the timer.
- **Information displays (S9 §5.8).** Modern displays may include control, not only indication. Incorrect display behavior must not block the safety function. One-way safety-to-display paths or safety-qualified displays and controls simplify the case. Two-way paths to nonsafety controls inherit the same isolation issues as other nonsafety-to-safety links. Inadvertent touch actions on touch displays must not prevent the safety function.
- **Remaining Clause 5 topics (S9 §§5.9-5.15).** Control of access, repair, identification (clear, unambiguous, revision-traceable to configuration control), auxiliary features, multi-unit stations, human factors considerations, and reliability are in the appendix's walk. Identification must tie revision level to configuration documentation of the changes that revision made. Reliability review stays alert to digital-specific failure modes already raised under single-failure and integrity.
- **Sense, execute, and power (S9 §§6-8).** For sense-and-command and execute-feature functional and design requirements, the appendix adds no guidance beyond IEEE Std 603-1991. Power source requirements defer to the IEEE Std 603 evaluation subsection.
- **SDOE close (S9 §9).** The appendix points to Regulatory Guide 1.152 for digital safety system guidance on establishing an SDOE. The criteria body for RG 1.152 Rev 4 is ch09; this appendix's job is to send the reviewer there, not to restate the RG.

## Key Concepts

- **This appendix is a review procedure, not an applicant methods guide.** RG 1.152 states acceptable criteria. Appendix 7.1-D states how staff examine an application of IEEE Std 7-4.3.2.
- **Edition printed here is 2003.** Historical 1982 appears once as ancestry. 2016 does not appear as this appendix's endorsed edition.
- **Clause order is the standard's order.** Empty digital add-ons still count: they tell the reviewer to stop and use the IEEE Std 603 path.
- **Quality review reuses the three-legged BTP 7-14 idea.** Plans, followed life cycle, acceptable outputs. Detailed plan and product scoring stays in ch02-ch07; this chapter only records that the appendix sends the reviewer there.
- **Communications independence is where many digital designs are won or lost.** One-way is simpler; two-way demands deterministic behavior and failure handling.
- **Diagnostics can hurt as well as help.** Review diagnostic complexity and diagnostic failure modes, not only operational code.
- **Hardware watchdog is a standing staff expectation in this appendix's diagnostic scheme.**
- **Licensing basis still wins for plant work.** Pack pins and appendix pins are reconstruction aids, not a substitute for the plant commitment.

## Key Takeaways

1. Appendix 7.1-D Rev 1 (August 2016, ML16019A114) is the pack body for staff evaluation of IEEE Std 7-4.3.2 application.
2. The appendix walks the 2003 standard's structure against IEEE Std 603-1991 and 10 CFR 50.55a(h), and sends empty digital clauses back to the IEEE Std 603 evaluation path.
3. Quality, qualification, integrity, independence, diagnostics, displays, and related Clause 5 topics are the main digital review load; sense, execute, and several other clauses add little beyond IEEE Std 603-1991.
4. SDOE detail is deferred to RG 1.152; criteria and SDOE body for Rev 4 are ch09.
5. This chapter cites IEEE Std 7-4.3.2-2003 because that is the edition the appendix prints. The 2016 endorsement is ch09 only.
6. The plant licensing basis, not this pack, decides which revision binds a given plant.
