# Chapter 3: Criteria III-VII: design, procurement, and document control

Sources: 10 CFR Part 50 Appendix B (eCFR), criteria III-VII; eCFR pin-check 2026-11-11 (title 10 current as of 2026-11-09).

## Core Idea

Criteria III to VII carry requirements from design intent into purchased supply: Criterion III controls design itself and its verification, Criterion IV puts requirements into procurement documents, Criterion V prescribes quality-affecting activities by documented instructions with acceptance criteria, Criterion VI controls document issuance and changes, and Criterion VII assures purchased material, equipment, and services conform to those documents. Together they cover the paper half of the program spine.

## Frameworks Introduced

- Translation chain. Design bases and applicable regulatory requirements are correctly translated into specifications, drawings, procedures, and instructions, and every later activity works from those documents.
- Independent verification. Design adequacy is verified by individuals or groups other than those who performed the original design, though they may be from the same organization.

## Key Concepts

- Criterion III Design Control. Measures assure applicable regulatory requirements and design bases (defined in § 50.2 and as specified in the license application), or functional design criteria (defined in § 53.020), translate correctly into specifications, drawings, procedures, and instructions; quality standards are specified and deviations controlled. Design verification uses design reviews, alternate or simplified calculational methods, or suitable test programs; prototype testing, where used in lieu of other verification, qualifies under most adverse design conditions. Measures apply to reactor physics, stress, thermal, hydraulic, and accident analyses; material compatibility; inservice inspection accessibility; and acceptance criteria delineation. Design changes, including field changes, receive measures commensurate with the original design and approval by the original design organization unless another responsible organization is designated.
- Criterion IV Procurement Document Control. Measures assure regulatory requirements, design bases or functional design criteria, and other quality requirements are suitably included or referenced in procurement documents for material, equipment, and services, whether purchased by the applicant or by contractors and subcontractors; procurement documents require contractors and subcontractors quality assurance programs consistent with pertinent appendix provisions.
- Criterion V Instructions, Procedures, and Drawings. Activities affecting quality are prescribed by documented instructions, procedures, or drawings appropriate to the circumstances and accomplished in accordance with them; documents include quantitative or qualitative acceptance criteria for determining important activities satisfactorily accomplished.
- Criterion VI Document Control. Measures control issuance of documents prescribing activities affecting quality, including changes: review for adequacy, approval for release by authorized personnel, distribution and use at the location where the activity is performed. Changes are reviewed and approved by the same organizations that performed the original review and approval unless another responsible organization is designated.
- Criterion VII Control Purchased Material, Equipment, and Services. Measures assure purchased items conform to procurement documents: source evaluation and selection, objective evidence of quality furnished, inspection at the contractor or subcontractor source, and examination of products upon delivery. Documentary evidence of conformance is available at the plant site before installation or use and retained there, sufficient to identify specific requirements (codes, standards, specifications) met. Effectiveness of control over contractors and subcontractors is assessed at intervals consistent with importance, complexity, and quantity.

## Mental Models

Read the five as one document pipeline: design (III) generates requirements, procurement (IV) copies them into purchase documents, instructions (V) carry them into execution, document control (VI) keeps the right version at the point of use, and purchased-item control (VII) checks suppliers against the same documents. Loss at any link breaks the chain.

## Anti-patterns

Merging the five criteria into one generic "paperwork" requirement; each addresses a distinct control point. Turning document control into filing advice real plant; the criterion requires review, approval, and distribution at point of use, not retention habits.

## Key Takeaways

- III: design bases translated under control; verification by others than original designers.
- IV: procurement documents carry quality requirements down the supply chain.
- V: documented instructions with acceptance criteria prescribe quality-affecting activities.
- VI: documents reviewed, approved, distributed, changed under control.
- VII: purchased conformity proven by evidence available at site before use.
