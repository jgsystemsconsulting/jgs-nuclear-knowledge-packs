# Chapter 3: Criteria III-VII: design, procurement, and document control

Sources: 10 CFR Part 50 Appendix B (eCFR), criteria III-VII; eCFR pin-check 2026-11-11 (title 10 current as of 2026-11-09).

## Core Idea

Criteria III to VII carry requirements from design intent into purchased supply. Criterion III controls the design itself and its verification, Criterion IV puts requirements into procurement documents, Criterion V prescribes quality-affecting activities by documented instructions with acceptance criteria, Criterion VI controls document issuance and changes, and Criterion VII assures that purchased material, equipment, and services conform to those documents. Together they cover the paper half of the program spine.

## Frameworks Introduced

- Translation chain. Applicable regulatory requirements and design bases are correctly translated into specifications, drawings, procedures, and instructions, and every later activity works from those documents.
- Independent verification. The adequacy of the design is verified or checked by individuals or groups other than those who performed the original design, though they may be from the same organization.

## Key Concepts

- Criterion III, Design Control. Measures assure that applicable regulatory requirements and the design bases (defined in § 50.2 and as specified in the license application), or the functional design criteria, as defined in § 53.020, are correctly translated into specifications, drawings, procedures, and instructions; that appropriate quality standards are specified in design documents and deviations from them controlled; and that the suitability of materials, parts, equipment, and processes essential to safety-related functions is selected and reviewed. Design interfaces are identified and coordinated among participating design organizations, with procedures for review, approval, release, distribution, and revision of interface documents. Verification uses design reviews, alternate or simplified calculational methods, or a suitable test program; where a test program verifies a specific design feature in lieu of other verification, it includes suitable qualification testing of a prototype unit under the most adverse design conditions. The measures apply to reactor physics, stress, thermal, hydraulic, and accident analyses; compatibility of materials; accessibility for inservice inspection, maintenance, and repair; and delineation of acceptance criteria for inspections and tests. Design changes, including field changes, receive measures commensurate with those applied to the original design and approval by the organization that performed the original design, unless the applicant designates another responsible organization.
- Criterion IV, Procurement Document Control. Measures assure that applicable regulatory requirements, design bases or functional design criteria, and other requirements necessary to assure adequate quality are suitably included or referenced in procurement documents for material, equipment, and services, whether purchased by the applicant or by its contractors or subcontractors. To the extent necessary, procurement documents require contractors and subcontractors to provide quality assurance programs consistent with the pertinent provisions of the appendix.
- Criterion V, Instructions, Procedures, and Drawings. Activities affecting quality are prescribed by documented instructions, procedures, or drawings of a type appropriate to the circumstances and are accomplished in accordance with them. Those documents include quantitative or qualitative acceptance criteria for determining that important activities have been satisfactorily accomplished.
- Criterion VI, Document Control. Measures control the issuance of documents, instructions, procedures, and drawings, including changes, that prescribe activities affecting quality. Documents, including changes, are reviewed for adequacy and approved for release by authorized personnel, and are distributed and used at the location where the prescribed activity is performed. Changes are reviewed and approved by the same organizations that performed the original review and approval, unless the applicant designates another responsible organization.
- Criterion VII, Control of Purchased Material, Equipment, and Services. Measures assure that purchased material, equipment, and services, whether purchased directly or through contractors and subcontractors, conform to the procurement documents. The measures include, as appropriate, source evaluation and selection, objective evidence of quality furnished by the contractor or subcontractor, inspection at the contractor or subcontractor source, and examination of products upon delivery. Documentary evidence that material and equipment conform to the procurement requirements is available at the plant site before installation or use, is retained there, and is sufficient to identify the specific requirements, such as codes, standards, or specifications, met by the purchased items. The effectiveness of the control over contractors and subcontractors is assessed by the applicant or its designee at intervals consistent with the importance, complexity, and quantity of the product or services.

## Mental Models

Read the five criteria as one document pipeline: design (III) generates requirements, procurement (IV) copies them into purchase documents, instructions (V) carry them into execution, document control (VI) keeps the right version at the point of use, and purchased-item control (VII) checks suppliers against the same documents. A loss at any link breaks the chain.

## Anti-patterns

Merging the five criteria into one generic paperwork requirement; each addresses a distinct control point. Turning document control into filing advice for a real plant; the criterion requires review, approval, and distribution at the point of use, not retention habits.

## Key Takeaways

- Criterion III: design bases translated under control; design verified by persons other than the original designers.
- Criterion IV: procurement documents carry quality requirements down the supply chain.
- Criterion V: documented instructions with acceptance criteria prescribe quality-affecting activities.
- Criterion VI: documents are reviewed, approved, distributed, and changed under control.
- Criterion VII: purchased conformity is proven by documentary evidence available at the site before use.
