# Chapter 5: Criteria XIII-XVIII: handling, status, corrective action, records, and audits

Sources: 10 CFR Part 50 Appendix B (eCFR), criteria XIII-XVIII; eCFR pin-check 2026-11-11 (title 10 current as of 2026-11-09).

## Core Idea

Criteria XIII to XVIII close the program loop: protect items in handling and storage, mark inspection, test, and operating status so nothing slips through, control nonconforming items, correct conditions adverse to quality, keep sufficient records, and audit the whole program periodically. They govern what happens to material and information after the design, procurement, and execution controls of criteria III to XII have acted.

## Frameworks Introduced

- Status as barrier. Markings such as stamps, tags, labels, routing cards, and valve and switch tags indicate status, so required inspections cannot be bypassed and equipment cannot be operated inadvertently.
- Feedback into the program. Nonconformances and conditions adverse to quality feed documented corrective action, and audits measure whether the program itself complies and remains effective.

## Key Concepts

- Criterion XIII, Handling, Storage and Shipping. Measures control the handling, storage, shipping, cleaning, and preservation of material and equipment, in accordance with work and inspection instructions, to prevent damage or deterioration. Where particular products require it, special protective environments, such as an inert gas atmosphere, specific moisture content levels, and temperature levels, are specified and provided.
- Criterion XIV, Inspection, Test, and Operating Status. Measures indicate, by the use of markings such as stamps, tags, labels, and routing cards, the status of inspections and tests performed upon individual items. Identification of items that have satisfactorily passed the required inspections and tests precludes inadvertent bypassing of the inspections and tests. Measures also indicate the operating status of structures, systems, and components, such as by tagging valves and switches, to prevent inadvertent operation.
- Criterion XV, Nonconforming Materials, Parts, or Components. Measures control materials, parts, or components that do not conform to requirements, to prevent their inadvertent use or installation. The measures include procedures for identification, documentation, segregation, disposition, and notification of affected organizations. Nonconforming items are reviewed and accepted, rejected, repaired, or reworked in accordance with documented procedures.
- Criterion XVI, Corrective Action. Measures assure that conditions adverse to quality, such as failures, malfunctions, deficiencies, deviations, defective material and equipment, and nonconformances, are promptly identified and corrected. In the case of a significant condition adverse to quality, the measures assure that the cause of the condition is determined and corrective action is taken to preclude repetition. The identification of the significant condition, the cause of the condition, and the corrective action taken are documented and reported to appropriate levels of management.
- Criterion XVII, Quality Assurance Records. Sufficient records are maintained to furnish evidence of activities affecting quality. The records include, at least, operating logs and the results of reviews, inspections, tests, audits, monitoring of work performance, and materials analyses, and also closely related data such as qualifications of personnel, procedures, and equipment. Inspection and test records identify, at a minimum, the inspector or data recorder, the type of observation, the results, the acceptability, and the action taken in connection with any deficiencies noted. Records are identifiable and retrievable. Consistent with applicable regulatory requirements, the applicant establishes requirements for record retention, such as duration, location, and assigned responsibility.
- Criterion XVIII, Audits. A comprehensive system of planned and periodic audits is carried out to verify compliance with all aspects of the quality assurance program and to determine the effectiveness of the program. The audits are performed in accordance with written procedures or checklists by appropriately trained personnel not having direct responsibilities in the areas audited. Audit results are documented and reviewed by management having responsibility, and follow-up action is taken where indicated.

## Mental Models

Read the six criteria as preservation, then verification, then proof: Criteria XIII and XIV keep good items good and visible, Criterion XV quarantines the bad ones, Criterion XVI drives lessons back into the program, Criterion XVII accumulates the evidence trail, and Criterion XVIII checks the program itself from outside. Audits under Criterion XVIII assess program compliance and effectiveness; corrective action under Criterion XVI fixes identified conditions. The two feed each other but remain distinct criteria.

## Anti-patterns

Merging Criteria XVIII and XVI into one generic fix-problems criterion; audit independence and corrective action root-cause duties are separate requirements. Treating records under Criterion XVII as retention trivia; records must be identifiable, retrievable evidence of quality-affecting activities, not archived paper.

## Key Takeaways

- Criterion XIII: handling and storage prevent damage or deterioration.
- Criterion XIV: status markings preclude bypass of inspections and inadvertent operation.
- Criterion XV: nonconforming items are segregated and dispositioned under documented procedures.
- Criterion XVI: significant conditions get cause determination and repetition-preventing action, reported to management.
- Criterion XVII: sufficient, identifiable, retrievable records evidence quality-affecting activities.
- Criterion XVIII: planned periodic audits by independent, trained personnel verify program compliance and effectiveness.
