# Chapter 5: Criteria XIII-XVIII: handling, status, corrective action, records, and audits

Sources: 10 CFR Part 50 Appendix B (eCFR), criteria XIII-XVIII; eCFR pin-check 2026-11-11 (title 10 current as of 2026-11-09).

## Core Idea

Criteria XIII to XVIII close the program loop: protect items in handling and storage, mark inspection, test, and operating status so nothing slips through, control nonconforming items, correct conditions adverse to quality, keep sufficient records, and audit the whole program periodically. They govern what happens to material and information after design, procurement, and execution controls have acted.

## Frameworks Introduced

- Status as barrier. Markings (stamps, tags, labels, routing cards, valve and switch tags) indicate status so inspections cannot be bypassed and equipment cannot be operated inadvertently.
- Feedback into the program. Nonconformances and adverse conditions feed documented corrective action, and audits measure whether the program itself complies and remains effective.

## Key Concepts

- Criterion XIII Handling, Storage and Shipping. Measures control handling, storage, shipping, cleaning, and preservation of material and equipment in accordance with work and inspection instructions to prevent damage or deterioration; special protective environments (inert gas atmosphere, specific moisture content levels, temperature levels) are specified and provided where particular products require.
- Criterion XIV Inspection, Test, and Operating Status. Measures indicate by markings the status of inspections and tests upon individual items; identification of items passed the required inspections and tests precludes inadvertent bypassing. Measures also indicate operating status of structures, systems, and components, such as by tagging valves and switches, to prevent inadvertent operation.
- Criterion XV Nonconforming Materials, Parts, or Components. Measures control items which do not conform to requirements to prevent inadvertent use or installation, including procedures for identification, documentation, segregation, disposition, and notification of affected organizations; nonconforming items are reviewed and accepted, rejected, repaired, or reworked in accordance with documented procedures.
- Criterion XVI Corrective Action. Conditions adverse to quality (failures, malfunctions, deficiencies, deviations, defective material and equipment, nonconformances) are promptly identified and corrected. For significant conditions adverse to quality, the cause is determined, corrective action is taken to preclude repetition, and the identification, cause, and corrective action taken are documented and reported to appropriate levels of management.
- Criterion XVII Quality Assurance Records. Sufficient records are maintained to furnish evidence of activities affecting quality: operating logs and results of reviews, inspections, tests, audits, monitoring of work performance, and materials analyses, plus closely related data such as personnel, procedures, and equipment qualifications. Inspection and test records identify, at minimum, the inspector or data recorder, type of observation, results, acceptability, and action taken on deficiencies noted. Records are identifiable and retrievable; the applicant establishes retention requirements (duration, location, assigned responsibility) consistent with applicable regulatory requirements.
- Criterion XVIII Audits. A comprehensive system of planned and periodic audits verifies compliance with all aspects of the quality assurance program and determines its effectiveness. Audits are performed per written procedures or checklists by appropriately trained personnel not having direct responsibilities in the areas audited; results are documented and reviewed by management having responsibility, and follow-up action is taken where indicated.

## Mental Models

Read the six as preservation then verification then proof: XIII and XIV keep good items good and visible, XV quarantines bad ones, XVI drives lessons back into the program, XVII accumulates the evidence trail, and XVIII checks the program itself from outside. Audits (XVIII) assess program compliance; corrective action (XVI) fixes identified conditions; the two feed each other but stay distinct criteria.

## Anti-patterns

Merging XVIII and XVI into one "fix problems" criterion; audit independence and corrective action root-cause duties are separate. Treating records under XVII as retention trivia; records must be identifiable and retrievable evidence, not archived paper.

## Key Takeaways

- XIII: handling and storage prevent damage or deterioration.
- XIV: status markings preclude bypass of inspections and inadvertent operation.
- XV: nonconforming items are segregated and dispositioned under documented procedures.
- XVI: significant conditions get cause determination and repetition-preventing action, reported to management.
- XVII: sufficient, identifiable, retrievable records evidence quality-affecting activities.
- XVIII: planned periodic audits by independent trained personnel verify program compliance and effectiveness.
