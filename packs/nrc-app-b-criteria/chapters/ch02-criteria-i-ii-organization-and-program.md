# Chapter 2: Criteria I-II: organization and QA program

Sources: 10 CFR Part 50 Appendix B (eCFR), criteria I-II; eCFR pin-check 2026-11-11 (title 10 current as of 2026-11-09).

## Core Idea

Criteria I and II are the program-bearing criteria. Criterion I places establishment and execution of the quality assurance program on the applicant and fixes who holds responsibility and authority. Criterion II requires the program established early, documented in writing, carried out throughout plant life, and regularly reviewed. Every remaining criterion of Appendix B operates inside the structure these two create.

## Frameworks Introduced

- Responsibility stays while work moves. The applicant may delegate program work to contractors, agents, or consultants, but retains responsibility for the quality assurance program.
- Assurance functions separate from doing. Quality assurance functions mean assuring that activities are appropriately performed and verifying, by checking, auditing, and inspecting, that activities affecting safety-related functions have been correctly performed.

## Key Concepts

- Criterion I, Organization. The applicant is responsible for establishing and executing the quality assurance program; delegation does not transfer that responsibility. The authority and duties of persons and organizations performing activities affecting the safety-related functions of structures, systems, and components are clearly established and delineated in writing. Persons and organizations performing quality assurance functions have sufficient authority and organizational freedom to identify quality problems, to initiate, recommend, or provide solutions, and to verify implementation of solutions. They report to a management level that assures that authority and organizational freedom, including sufficient independence from cost and schedule when they are opposed to safety considerations.
- Criterion II, Quality Assurance Program. The applicant establishes the program at the earliest practicable time, consistent with the schedule for accomplishing activities, documented by written policies, procedures, or instructions and carried out throughout plant life in accordance with them. The program identifies the structures, systems, and components it covers and the major organizations participating in it, together with their designated functions. Control over activities affecting quality is provided to an extent consistent with the importance to safety, and those activities are accomplished under suitably controlled conditions: appropriate equipment, suitable environmental conditions, and assurance that all prerequisites for the activity have been satisfied. The program takes into account the need for special controls, processes, test equipment, tools, and skills to attain the required quality, and provides indoctrination and training as necessary to assure that suitable proficiency is achieved and maintained. The applicant regularly reviews the status and adequacy of the program, and management of each participating organization regularly reviews its own part.

## Mental Models

Read the pair as frame and engine: Criterion I answers who owns quality assurance and who may verify independently of cost and schedule pressure; Criterion II answers what exists on paper and how it stays alive over plant life. Criteria III to XVIII are program content that this pair must carry.

## Anti-patterns

Turning the criteria into organization-chart or program-design advice; the criteria require structure, delineation, and review, not a specific chart shape. Reading Criterion II as approval of any particular QA program description; a facility's program binds through its own licence documents, not through this pack.

## Key Takeaways

- Criterion I: applicant responsibility, written delineation of authority and duties, quality assurance functions with organizational freedom and independence from cost and schedule.
- Criterion II: a documented program established at the earliest practicable time, carried out throughout plant life, scaled to importance to safety, and regularly reviewed.
