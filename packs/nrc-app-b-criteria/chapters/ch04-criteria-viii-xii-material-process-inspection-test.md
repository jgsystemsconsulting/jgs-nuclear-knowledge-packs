# Chapter 4: Criteria VIII-XII: material, process, inspection, and test

Sources: 10 CFR Part 50 Appendix B (eCFR), criteria VIII-XII; eCFR pin-check 2026-11-11 (title 10 current as of 2026-11-09).

## Core Idea

Criteria VIII to XII control the physical work: identification of items so the wrong or defective part is never used, qualification of the persons and procedures behind special processes, inspection by persons other than the performers, a test program that identifies every required test, and calibration of the measuring and test equipment the evidence rests on. These five criteria exercise the material and measurement half of the program spine.

## Frameworks Introduced

- Traceable identity. Every item carries identification, on itself or in records traceable to it, maintained through fabrication, erection, installation, and use.
- Verification by separation. Inspections are performed by individuals other than those who performed the activity inspected.

## Key Concepts

- Criterion VIII, Identification and Control of Materials, Parts, and Components. Measures are established for the identification and control of materials, parts, and components, including partially fabricated assemblies. Identification of the item is maintained with a heat number, part number, serial number, or other appropriate means, either on the item or on records traceable to the item, as required throughout fabrication, erection, installation, and use. The measures are designed to prevent the use of incorrect or defective material, parts, and components.
- Criterion IX, Control of Special Processes. Measures assure that special processes, including welding, heat treating, and nondestructive testing, are controlled and accomplished by qualified personnel using qualified procedures in accordance with applicable codes, standards, specifications, criteria, and other special requirements.
- Criterion X, Inspection. A program of inspection of activities affecting quality is established and executed by or for the organization performing the activity, to verify conformance with the documented instructions, procedures, and drawings for accomplishing the activity. Examinations are performed by individuals other than those who performed the activity inspected. Examinations, measurements, or tests of material or products processed are performed for each work operation where necessary to assure quality. Where a direct inspection of processed material or products is impossible or disadvantageous, indirect control by monitoring processing methods, equipment, and personnel is provided, and both the inspection and the process monitoring are provided when control is inadequate without both. Where mandatory inspection hold points require witnessing or inspecting by the applicant's designated representative, beyond which the work does not proceed without that consent, the specific hold points are indicated in the appropriate documents.
- Criterion XI, Test Control. A test program assures that all testing required to demonstrate that structures, systems, and components will perform satisfactorily in service is identified and performed in accordance with written test procedures incorporating the requirements and acceptance limits contained in applicable design documents. The program includes, as appropriate, proof tests before installation, preoperational tests, and operational tests during plant operation. Test procedures assure that prerequisites for a given test have been met, that adequate test instrumentation is available and used, and that the test is performed under suitable environmental conditions. Test results are documented and evaluated to assure that test requirements have been satisfied.
- Criterion XII, Control of Measuring and Test Equipment. Measures assure that tools, gages, instruments, and other measuring and testing devices used in activities affecting quality are properly controlled, calibrated, and adjusted at specified periods to maintain accuracy within necessary limits.

## Mental Models

Read the group as identity, then proof: Criterion VIII keeps the right part in place, Criterion IX keeps risky processes in qualified hands, Criteria X and XI generate the objective evidence that items and their tests meet documented criteria, and Criterion XII keeps the instruments behind that evidence trustworthy. Inspection under Criterion X verifies work already done; testing under Criterion XI demonstrates behavior against design-document acceptance limits.

## Anti-patterns

Reading inspection and test as interchangeable; Criterion X verifies conformance during and after work, while Criterion XI runs a program of identified tests. Treating Criterion XII as a calibration procedure template; it requires control and scheduled accuracy, not a specific calibration document form.

## Key Takeaways

- Criterion VIII: traceable identification prevents the use of incorrect or defective items.
- Criterion IX: special processes demand qualified personnel and qualified procedures.
- Criterion X: inspection verifies by persons other than the performers, with hold points where mandatory.
- Criterion XI: required tests are identified, procedured, and their results evaluated.
- Criterion XII: measuring and test equipment is controlled and calibrated at specified periods.
