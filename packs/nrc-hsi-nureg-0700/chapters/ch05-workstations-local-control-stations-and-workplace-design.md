# Chapter 5: Workstations, Local Control Stations, and Workplace Design

Sources: NRC NUREG-0700 Revision 4, Human-System Interface Design Review Guidelines, January 2026, ML26022A094; pin verified via official NRC publication page on 2026-11-13.

## Core Idea

Part III review covers where operators work: control room workstation design, local control stations outside the main control room, and control room environment. Lighting and acoustics are named environmental topics, and anthropometric fit is the discipline behind reach and viewing questions. The chapter names topic families only; numeric tables are explicitly out of pack scope, and no dimension, envelope, distance, illuminance, or noise value ships here.

## Frameworks Introduced

- Control room workstation design as review topic family. How displays and controls are arranged at operator positions is a review question; no console dimension or layout ships here.
- Local control stations as review topic family. Stations outside the main control room where operators act stay inside review; no station design ships here.
- Control room environment as review topic family. Lighting and acoustics are named environmental review topics; no illuminance or noise value ships here.
- Anthropometric fit as underlying discipline. Reach, viewing, and clearance questions rest on human body fit; numeric tables are explicitly out of pack scope, and no reach envelope or viewing distance ships here.
- Remote shutdown facilities as workplace location concept only. Shutdown stations outside the main control room are named as places operators work, and post-fire shutdown capability stays with the live fire protection pack; no capability position ships here.

## Key Concepts

- Workstation: the operator position where displays and controls are arranged for use; named here as a review topic, with no dimension or layout stated.
- Local control station: a station outside the main control room where operators monitor or act; named here as a review topic, with no design stated.
- Control room environment: lighting, acoustics, and surrounding conditions operators work in; named here as a review topic family, with no value stated.
- Lighting: environmental review topic covering workplace illumination questions; named here as a topic, with no illuminance value stated.
- Acoustics: environmental review topic covering workplace sound questions; named here as a topic, with no noise value stated.
- Anthropometry: discipline of human body fit behind reach, viewing, and clearance questions; named here as a discipline, with no table or value stated.
- Remote shutdown facility: a workplace location outside the main control room for shutdown operations; named here as a location concept only, with capability questions owned by the live fire protection pack.

## Mental Models

Read the workplace as the setting reviewers check around the HSI: positions where operators sit or stand, stations outside the main room, and the environment surrounding both. Keep arrangement, environment, and fit at topic level: review asks whether layout, lighting, and acoustics support operators and whether reach and viewing fit the crew, without stating any value this pack can answer from. When a candidate detail looks like a console dimension, a reach envelope, a viewing distance, an illuminance value, a noise value, or a drawn layout, it belongs in the guideline document, not in this pack.

## Anti-patterns

Stating any console dimension, reach envelope, viewing distance, illuminance value, noise value, numeric table, workplace layout, or guideline wording is this chapter's named anti-pattern; topic families ship, and no value or layout does. A companion error treats remote shutdown naming as a capability position; shutdown stations ship as workplace locations only, and post-fire shutdown capability stays with the live fire protection pack. A third error treats a topic definition as design instruction for a real control room; definitions state review topics for orientation, and no workplace layout is drawn or judged here. Numeric design values and guideline-level criteria are not packaged here; the guideline document itself is the source for any design or review decision.
