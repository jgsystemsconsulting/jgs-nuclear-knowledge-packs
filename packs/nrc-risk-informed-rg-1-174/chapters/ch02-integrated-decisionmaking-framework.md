# Chapter 2: Integrated decisionmaking framework

Sources: NRC RG 1.174 Rev. 3, An Approach for Using Probabilistic Risk Assessment in Risk-Informed Decisions on Plant-Specific Changes to the Licensing Basis, 2018, ML17317A256; pin 2026-11-08 (P9 VERIFY cleared).

## Core Idea

The guide works through integrated decisionmaking: probabilistic risk assessment insights are one input, combined with defense-in-depth, safety margins, and performance-based consideration, and the decision weighs those inputs together rather than resting on PRA numbers alone. The guide's stages give the framework its shape: define the proposed change, engineering analysis, integrated decisionmaking, implementation and monitoring.

## Frameworks Introduced

- The four stages. A proposed change is defined first, then analyzed in the engineering analysis stage (ch03), then decided through integrated decisionmaking, then carried through implementation and monitoring (ch07). The stages run in that order in the guide.
- Inputs weighed together. PRA insights, defense-in-depth, safety margins, and performance-based consideration are the framework's considerations; integrated decisionmaking is the act of weighing them together, not summing them into one number.

## Key Concepts

- Define the proposed change: the stage where what is being changed is stated before any analysis runs.
- Engineering analysis: the stage where the change-in-risk work happens, with the metrics named in ch03.
- Integrated decisionmaking: the weighing stage, where PRA insights stand alongside defense-in-depth, safety margins, and performance-based consideration.
- Implementation and monitoring: the stage that follows an approved change (ch07).
- Defense-in-depth and safety margins are maintained through the change (ch05); performance-based consideration is part of the framework (ch05).

## Mental Models

Read the framework as a set of parallel inputs meeting at one decision point. The PRA result does not carry the decision by itself: the change also has to hold up on defense-in-depth, on safety margins, and on the performance-based side, and integrated decisionmaking is where those threads are weighed together.

## Anti-patterns

Deciding on PRA numbers alone is the chapter's named anti-pattern: the framework names PRA insights as one input among several, and a decision that weighs nothing else has left the framework. A companion error is collapsing the stages, running the weighing before the engineering analysis that feeds it.

## Key Takeaways

- The guide's stages: define the proposed change, engineering analysis, integrated decisionmaking, implementation and monitoring.
- PRA insights are one input, combined with defense-in-depth, safety margins, and performance-based consideration.
- Integrated decisionmaking weighs those inputs together rather than PRA numbers alone.
