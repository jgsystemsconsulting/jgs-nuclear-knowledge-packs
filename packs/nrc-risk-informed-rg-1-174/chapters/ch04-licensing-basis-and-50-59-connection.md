# Chapter 4: Licensing basis and 50.59 connection

Sources: NRC RG 1.174 Rev. 3, An Approach for Using Probabilistic Risk Assessment in Risk-Informed Decisions on Plant-Specific Changes to the Licensing Basis, 2018, ML17317A256; 10 CFR 50 section 50.59 for the carried B-01 facts; pin 2026-11-08 (P9 VERIFY cleared).

## Core Idea

A risk-informed change to the licensing basis sits next to a bounded lane in 10 CFR 50 section 50.59: the no-amendment change lane. The Part 50 licensing basis pack carries the 50.59 change lane this framework connects to. This chapter orients where the two meet, using three carried facts and nothing more; it is orientation, not screening advice and not legal advice.

## Frameworks Introduced

- The two-limb gate. A no-amendment change under 50.59 must pass both limbs: a technical specification amendment is not required, and none of the eight paragraph (c)(2) criteria applies.
- The complement, stated as orientation only: if either limb fails, the change does not qualify under that lane.

## Key Concepts

- The threshold wording in the paragraph (c)(2) criteria is "more than a minimal increase"; this pack carries that wording and no criterion list.
- The screening baseline is the FSAR (as updated), updated per 50.71(e) or (f); screening runs against that updated document.
- Where a risk-informed change sits: the 50.59 lane asks its own two-limb question about whether a change needs no amendment, while the RG 1.174 framework weighs PRA insights together with defense-in-depth, safety margins, and performance-based consideration for a plant-specific change to the licensing basis. The two questions are distinct, and a change question can touch both.
- Scope limit: this chapter carries no criterion list, no screening procedure, and no records or reporting content from 50.59; those belong to the Part 50 licensing basis pack.

## Mental Models

Hold the two frames apart. The 50.59 lane is a bounded gate with a fixed baseline, the FSAR (as updated); the risk-informed framework is a weighing framework with named inputs and named change-in-risk metrics (ch03). A change can raise both questions at once, and confusing one frame for the other misreads both: the gate does not weigh inputs, and the framework does not screen.

## Anti-patterns

Reading this chapter as screening advice or legal advice is the named anti-pattern: the chapter states where the frameworks meet, never whether a real change passes either limb or how to run a screening. A companion error is reconstructing the eight criteria from memory; this pack carries the threshold wording and the gate shape only.

## Key Takeaways

- The 50.59 no-amendment lane requires both limbs to pass: no technical specification amendment required, and none of the eight paragraph (c)(2) criteria applies.
- The threshold wording is "more than a minimal increase"; the screening baseline is the FSAR (as updated), per 50.71(e) or (f).
- If either limb fails, the change does not qualify under that lane.
- This connection is orientation only: not screening advice, not legal advice, no criterion list, no screening procedure.
