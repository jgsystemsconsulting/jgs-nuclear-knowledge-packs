# Chapter 5: Post-Fire Safe Shutdown Capability and Circuit Analysis

Sources: NRC RG 1.189 Rev 5, Fire Protection for Nuclear Power Plants, October 2023, ML23214A287; verified via Federal Register notice 88 FR 76989 on 2026-11-13.

## Core Idea

Post-fire safe shutdown capability means the plant can still perform reactivity control, coolant inventory and pressure control, decay heat removal, vital auxiliary support, and process monitoring after a fire. Protection rests on keeping one redundant train free of fire damage, by separation or barriers, and on circuit analysis, because fire-damaged associated circuits can cause spurious operation or loss of control outside the fire area. This pack defines the concepts only; no train, circuit, or plant verdict is stated. Every protection claim stays in question form: the analysis must answer whether one train remains free of fire damage.

## Frameworks Introduced

- Safe shutdown function set. Reactivity control, coolant inventory and pressure control, decay heat removal, vital auxiliaries, and process monitoring name what the plant must still do after a fire.
- Redundant train protection as a question. Separation and barriers are the protection idea; the analysis question is whether one train remains free of fire damage, not a packaged verdict that any train remains free of fire damage.
- Circuit analysis reason for being. Fire can damage cables and circuits in ways that defeat equipment without burning the equipment itself, including spurious operation and loss of control or indication outside the fire area.
- Concept set only. Safe shutdown equipment, associated circuit, and spurious operation are defined as terms; no example fire area, train, cable, or worked case is packaged, and no Appendix R compliance verdict appears.

## Key Concepts

- Post-fire safe shutdown capability: ability to achieve and maintain safe shutdown after a fire by performing the named safe shutdown functions; concept only, with no plant verdict stated.
- Reactivity control: function that keeps the reactor subcritical as part of safe shutdown; named function only.
- Coolant inventory and pressure control: function that keeps coolant inventory and pressure within the band needed for safe shutdown; named function only.
- Decay heat removal: function that removes decay heat after shutdown; named function only.
- Vital auxiliaries: support functions needed so the safe shutdown functions can run; named function only.
- Process monitoring: indication and monitoring needed to know plant state during post-fire safe shutdown; named function only.
- Safe shutdown equipment: equipment relied on to achieve and maintain safe shutdown after a fire; named concept only, with no equipment list or train verdict here.
- Associated circuit: a circuit that is not itself a safe shutdown circuit but whose fire damage can affect safe shutdown equipment through shared power, control, or spurious operation paths; concept only.
- Spurious operation: unwanted start, stop, or misposition of equipment caused by fire damage to circuits; concept only, with no worked case stated.
- Redundant train separation and barriers: protection idea that keeps one train free of fire damage by physical separation or by barriers; stated as the question an analysis must answer, with no separation distance or barrier rating stated.

## Mental Models

Read safe shutdown capability as the third leg of the stool: even when a fire starts and grows, the plant must still shut down and stay shut down. Picture two redundant trains that can each perform the function set. The protection idea is not that both trains survive every fire; the analysis question is whether at least one train remains free of fire damage for the fire under study. Circuit analysis exists because fire damage is not only heat on equipment: damaged associated circuits can spuriously operate gear or drop control and indication in spaces outside the fire area. This chapter names the functions and the questions; it does not analyse a train, draw a fire area, work a spurious operation case, or issue a compliance verdict.

## Anti-patterns

Issuing a train, circuit, or plant verdict from these notes is the named anti-pattern: every protection claim stays in question form. A companion error is treating a worked example fire area, train, or cable as packaged here; none is. A third error is reading suppression success from ch04 as proof of safe shutdown capability; ch04 limits growth and duration and does not close this question. A fourth error is stating a separation distance, barrier rating, or Appendix R compliance verdict for any plant from orientation notes. A fifth error is collapsing this chapter into alternative or dedicated shutdown; that fallback lives in ch06 when redundant train separation cannot be assured.

## Key Takeaways

- Post-fire safe shutdown capability means the plant can still perform reactivity control, coolant inventory and pressure control, decay heat removal, vital auxiliaries, and process monitoring after a fire.
- Protection rests on keeping one redundant train free of fire damage by separation or barriers; that claim is an analysis question, not a verdict packaged here.
- Circuit analysis exists because fire-damaged associated circuits can cause spurious operation or loss of control and indication outside the fire area.
