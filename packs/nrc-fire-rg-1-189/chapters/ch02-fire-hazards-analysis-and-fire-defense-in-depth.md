# Chapter 2: Fire Hazards Analysis and Fire Defense-in-Depth

Sources: NRC RG 1.189 Rev 5, Fire Protection for Nuclear Power Plants, October 2023, ML23214A287; verified via Federal Register notice 88 FR 76989 on 2026-11-13.

## Core Idea

A fire hazards analysis identifies, area by area, the fire hazards present, the fire protection features provided, and the effect of postulated fires on the ability to achieve and maintain safe shutdown. A fire protection program works three goals at once: prevent fires from starting; rapidly detect, control, and extinguish fires that do occur; and provide protection so a fire cannot prevent safe shutdown. Those three goals are this pack's definition of fire defense-in-depth and are evaluated as a balance; no single leg carries the whole safety case. This chapter defines the analysis idea and the doctrine at orientation depth only: no fire hazards analysis is performed here, no fire area is drawn, no rating or loading value is stated, and no plant conclusion is given.

## Frameworks Introduced

- Fire hazards analysis as orientation idea. Area-by-area identification of hazards, protection features, and postulated-fire effects on safe shutdown capability. The chapter names what the analysis is for; it does not run one.
- Three defense-in-depth goals in balance. Prevent; detect, control, and extinguish promptly; protect so fire cannot prevent safe shutdown. Balance means each leg is judged with the other two in view, not as three independent checklists that can pass alone.
- Concept set only. Fire area, fire barrier, combustible loading, and safe shutdown equipment are defined as terms; no boundary, rating, loading number, or equipment list is packaged.
- Sense marker. Defense-in-depth here is fire doctrine. It is distinct from the cyber defense-in-depth sense carried by the live RG 5.71 pack and from the non-LWR defense-in-depth sense carried by the live RG 1.233 pack; those packs keep their own senses. The live RG 1.174 pack carries defense-in-depth as a risk-informed decision consideration for licensing-basis changes; that sense stays there.

## Key Concepts

- Fire hazards analysis: plant-wide, area-by-area look at hazards present, protection features provided, and effect of postulated fires on safe shutdown capability; concept only in this pack.
- Fire defense-in-depth: the three program goals evaluated in balance (prevent; detect, control, and extinguish; protect safe shutdown capability).
- Fire area: a plant space treated as a unit for fire protection analysis; named as a concept only, with no boundary drawn here.
- Fire barrier: a feature that limits fire spread between spaces or protects equipment; named as a concept only, with no rating stated here.
- Combustible loading: the combustible material present in a space, treated as an analysis input concept; no loading value is stated here.
- Safe shutdown equipment: equipment relied on to achieve and maintain safe shutdown after a fire; named as a concept only, with no equipment list or train verdict here.
- PRA neighbour hook (prose only): the live RG 1.200 pack carries PRA technical adequacy orientation; this pack states only that a performance-based fire protection basis presumes adequate fire risk capability and does not restate adequacy.

## Mental Models

Picture three legs of one stool. Prevention keeps fires from starting. Detection and suppression limit growth and duration when a fire starts. Protection of safe shutdown capability keeps the plant able to shut down even if a fire still grows. Remove any leg and the stool tips; checking one leg alone never proves the program. Read a fire hazards analysis as the map that asks, space by space, what can burn, what protection sits there, and what a fire would do to safe shutdown. This chapter names the map; it does not fill one in for any plant. When the words defense-in-depth appear here, read the fire sense only; cyber and non-LWR senses live in their own packs.

## Anti-patterns

Treating any single defense-in-depth leg as the whole safety case is the chapter's named anti-pattern: the three goals are judged in balance. A companion error is reading this chapter as a fire hazards analysis for a real plant, drawing a fire area, stating a barrier rating or combustible loading value, or issuing a plant conclusion from these notes. A third error is collapsing fire defense-in-depth into the cyber sense from the live RG 5.71 pack, the non-LWR sense from the live RG 1.233 pack, or the risk-informed decision sense from the live RG 1.174 pack. A fourth error is restating PRA technical adequacy method from this pack; adequacy stays with the live RG 1.200 pack.

## Key Takeaways

- A fire hazards analysis identifies hazards, protection features, and postulated-fire effects on safe shutdown capability area by area; this pack defines the idea and performs no analysis.
- Fire defense-in-depth is the three goals in balance: prevent; detect, control, and extinguish; protect so fire cannot prevent safe shutdown.
- No single leg carries the safety case.
- Fire area, fire barrier, combustible loading, and safe shutdown equipment are concepts only here; no rating, loading value, area boundary, or plant conclusion is stated.
- Defense-in-depth in this pack is fire doctrine, distinct from cyber and non-LWR senses in live sibling packs named in prose.
