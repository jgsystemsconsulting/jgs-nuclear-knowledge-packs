# Chapter 4: Categorization and design criteria

Sources: NRC RG 1.97 Rev. 5, Criteria for Accident Monitoring Instrumentation for Nuclear Power Plants, May 2019, ML18136A762 (Federal Register notice 2019-08819, 84 FR 18591); pin 2026-11-09 (dual-source verified).

## Core Idea

The guide frames design criteria and a categorization of monitored variables. The frame is what this pack carries: category labels, criteria text, and any design detail live in the guide and the endorsed standard and are not reproduced here. This chapter is descriptive orientation only, and it contains no instrumentation design how-to.

## Frameworks Introduced

- The categorization frame. The guide sorts monitored variables into a categorization; the sort supports how criteria apply. Category labels beyond the older-revision Type and Category note from the pin live in the guide and the endorsed standard.
- The design-criteria frame. The guide frames design criteria for accident monitoring instrumentation; the criteria text lives in the guide and the endorsed standard. This pack states that the criteria exist and frames their subject matter, and carries none of their content.

## Key Concepts

- Categorization: the guide's sorting of monitored variables; the labels are a guide fact, not a pack fact. Older revisions used Type and Category variable schemes tied to plant licensing bases.
- Design criteria: the criteria frame the guide presents for the instrumentation; the text lives in the guide and the endorsed standard.
- No design how-to: no sensor, range, qualification, display, or configuration prescription appears in this pack.
- Variable lists, category criteria, and design detail live in RG 1.97 Rev. 5 and the endorsed standard; they are not reproduced here.

## Mental Models

Read categorization as the guide's way of sorting the monitored-variable subject matter, and design criteria as the guide's way of framing what the instrumentation must do, both at frame level. A frame tells you the shape of the answer; the guide holds the answer. When a question asks which category a variable falls in or what a design criterion requires, the guide and the endorsed standard are the sources.

## Anti-patterns

Reading this chapter as a design method is the chapter's named anti-pattern: orientation notes frame the criteria, they do not prescribe instrumentation. A companion error is reconstructing category labels or criteria text from memory; both live in the guide and the endorsed standard, and neither appears here.
