# Chapter 3: Variables and selection criteria

Sources: NRC RG 1.97 Rev. 5, Criteria for Accident Monitoring Instrumentation for Nuclear Power Plants, May 2019, ML18136A762 (Federal Register notice 2019-08819, 84 FR 18591); pin 2026-11-09 (dual-source verified).

## Core Idea

The guide frames which variables are monitored and the criteria used to select them. The frame is the whole of what this pack carries: specific variable lists, ranges, and selection outcomes live in the guide and are not stated here. Rev. 5 also adds severe-accident-management-guideline variables to the monitored-variable subject matter, and Rev. 5 is technology-neutral in how it frames that subject matter.

## Frameworks Introduced

- The variables frame. Accident monitoring instrumentation provides the control-room operator information about plant conditions during and after accidents, so the guide's selection question is which plant conditions the instrumentation should show the operator. RG 1.97 presents criteria for making that selection; the variables themselves and their ranges live in the guide.
- The Rev. 5 addition. Rev. 5 adds severe-accident-management-guideline variables; the wording and reach of that addition live in the guide. This pack names the addition and carries none of its content.

## Key Concepts

- Monitored variables: the plant conditions the instrumentation shows the operator, framed by the guide; the list of variables is a guide fact, not a pack fact.
- Selection criteria: the criteria RG 1.97 presents for choosing monitored variables; the criteria text lives in the guide.
- Severe-accident-management-guideline variables: the variable family Rev. 5 adds; named here as an addition only.
- Variable lists, category criteria, and design detail live in RG 1.97 Rev. 5 and the endorsed standard; they are not reproduced here.

## Mental Models

Read the guide's variables chapter as the inventory and these notes as the statement that an inventory exists. A framework statement can tell you where an answer lives; it cannot be the answer. When a question names a variable, a range, or a selection outcome, the guide is the source and this pack has reached its edge.

## Anti-patterns

Treating the framework statement as a variable list, or as a selection answer for a named plant, is the chapter's named anti-pattern: the statement says the guide frames variables and selection criteria, and nothing more. Inventing a variable or a range from memory to fill the gap is the same failure one step worse.
