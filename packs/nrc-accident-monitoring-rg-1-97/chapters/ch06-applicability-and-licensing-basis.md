# Chapter 6: Applicability and licensing basis

Sources: NRC RG 1.97 Rev. 5, Criteria for Accident Monitoring Instrumentation for Nuclear Power Plants, May 2019, ML18136A762 (Federal Register notice 2019-08819, 84 FR 18591); pin 2026-11-09 (dual-source verified).

## Core Idea

Rev. 5 is technology-neutral and extends applicability language for 10 CFR Part 52; the wording and reach of that language live in the guide. On the licensing side, older revisions used Type and Category variable schemes tied to plant licensing bases, and many operating plants still reference an earlier revision in the current licensing basis. An older revision named in a plant's current licensing basis remains that plant's reference until the licensing record changes. Many operating plants reference an older RG 1.97 revision in the current licensing basis; read the governing revision from the plant record.

## Frameworks Introduced

- Technology-neutral applicability. Rev. 5 frames its criteria without leaning on one technology, and it extends applicability language for 10 CFR Part 52. The pack carries the frame and the fact of the extension; the language itself lives in the guide.
- The plant record rules. Because many operating plants reference an older revision in the current licensing basis, the revision that governs a named plant is read from that plant's licensing record, not assumed to be Rev. 5. The current revision of a guide and the revision a plant's licensing basis cites are two different facts.

## Key Concepts

- Technology-neutral: the Rev. 5 frame word for guide coverage that does not lean on one plant technology; the reach of that neutrality is stated in the guide.
- 10 CFR Part 52 extension: the applicability-language extension Rev. 5 adds; the wording lives in the guide.
- Current licensing basis: the licensing record a plant works from; it may name an older RG 1.97 revision, and that reference stands until the record changes.
- Governing-revision rule: read the governing revision for a named plant from that plant's licensing record, never from the guide's latest edition.

## Mental Models

Keep two clocks separate: the guide's revision clock (Rev. 4 in June 2006, Rev. 5 in May 2019) and a plant's licensing clock. The guide's clock says what is current on the shelf; the plant's clock says what that plant committed to. A question about which revision governs a named plant is a licensing-record question, and this pack carries no plant records.

## Anti-patterns

Assuming Rev. 5 governs every plant is the chapter's named anti-pattern: many operating plants reference an older revision, and only the plant record decides. This chapter is orientation; it is not legal advice, and it makes no plant-specific applicability determination.

## Key Takeaways

- Rev. 5 is technology-neutral and extends applicability language for 10 CFR Part 52; the wording lives in the guide.
- An older revision named in a plant's current licensing basis remains that plant's reference until the licensing record changes.
- The revision that governs a named plant is read from that plant's licensing record, not assumed to be Rev. 5.
