# Chapter 1: RG 1.97 role and scope

Sources: NRC RG 1.97 Rev. 5, Criteria for Accident Monitoring Instrumentation for Nuclear Power Plants, May 2019, ML18136A762 (Federal Register notice 2019-08819, 84 FR 18591); pin 2026-11-09 (dual-source verified).

## Core Idea

RG 1.97 is Nuclear Regulatory Commission guidance titled Criteria for Accident Monitoring Instrumentation for Nuclear Power Plants. Rev. 5 (May 2019, accession ML18136A762) is the current revision this pack pins, and it was issued through Federal Register notice 2019-08819 (84 FR 18591, May 1, 2019). The guide presents criteria for accident monitoring instrumentation for nuclear power plants. Accident monitoring instrumentation provides the control-room operator information about plant conditions during and after accidents. This pack orients the guide's framework; the guide text itself, not these notes, is what a reader works from.

## Frameworks Introduced

- Orientation, not the guide. The pack carries synthesized notes on what the guide covers and how its subject is framed: criteria for the instrumentation that shows the operator what the plant is doing during and after accidents. The guide's own text stays in the guide.
- Information for the operator. The frame this pack carries is two statements connected: accident monitoring instrumentation provides the control-room operator information about plant conditions during and after accidents, and RG 1.97 presents criteria for that instrumentation. The guide is an orientation entry point here and does not replace the guide text or a plant licensing record.

## Mental Models

Read the guide as the criteria source, not as a background document: anything that looks like a variable list, a category definition, a range, or a design prescription belongs to the guide and the endorsed standard, which this pack does not reproduce. Read these notes as a map of where the questions live, then take the question itself to the guide.

## Anti-patterns

Reading these orientation notes as a substitute for the guide text or a plant licensing record is the chapter's named anti-pattern: the notes locate and frame, the sources decide. A companion error is asking these notes for design detail; the pack answers none.
