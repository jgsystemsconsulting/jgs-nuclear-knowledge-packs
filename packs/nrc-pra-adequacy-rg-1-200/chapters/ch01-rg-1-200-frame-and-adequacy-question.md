# Chapter 1: RG 1.200 frame and adequacy question

Sources: NRC RG 1.200 Rev. 3, Acceptability Probabilistic Risk Assessment Results Risk-Informed Activities, December 2020, ML20238B871; listing-page pin 2026-11-08, re-checked 2026-11-11.

## Core Idea

RG 1.200 addresses the acceptability of PRA results used to support risk-informed activities. Its subject is whether the PRA behind such a use is technically adequate for the application. This pack orients that question from pin facts and closed statements; it carries no adequacy criteria, because those live in the guide. A Regulatory Guide describes one method the NRC staff finds acceptable; it is guidance, not regulation, and its text binds no licensee through this pack.

## Frameworks Introduced

- The RG 1.200 frame: an NRC regulatory guide, Rev. 3, December 2020, titled Acceptability of Probabilistic Risk Assessment Results for Risk-Informed Activities, describing one method the staff finds acceptable for judging the acceptability of PRA results.
- The adequacy question frame: before a PRA result is leaned on, the reader names the application and the decision the PRA would inform; adequacy is then a question about that named use, not about the PRA in the abstract.

## Key Concepts

- Pin facts: NRC RG 1.200 Rev. 3, December 2020, accession ML20238B871; Federal Register notice 2020-28632, published 2020-12-29; listing-page pin 2026-11-08, re-checked 2026-11-11.
- Later revisions of the guide are out of scope for this pack. Verify any point against the current guide listing before relying on it.
- Guidance, not regulation: the guide describes an acceptable method; it does not bind a licensee, and this pack adds no binding force.
- The subject line: acceptability of PRA results for risk-informed activities; the operative question is whether the PRA behind a use is technically adequate for the application.

## Mental Models

Name the application and the decision first; only then ask whether the PRA is adequate. Adequacy talk without a named use has nothing to be judged against, so the naming step comes before any adequacy claim in this pack.

## Anti-patterns

Treating this frame chapter as the guide itself is the named anti-pattern: the pack orients the question and pins facts, it does not restate guide content. A companion error is starting adequacy talk without a named application; if no use is named, stop and ask what the PRA would inform before going on.
